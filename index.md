I am Ginger. I am eight years old, I am a cat, and this is my website. The
hoomans built it because I told them to, using the ancient technique of
sitting on the keyboard until something happened.

Here is the part of the story I am told I was too small to remember. When I was
two weeks old — a period of my life I spent being approximately the size of a
bread roll and roughly as coordinated — my mother had a conversation with a
hooman. Cats do not write wills, so she did the next best thing and made it
extremely clear that this hooman was now responsible for me. Shortly afterwards
there was an accident, and she was gone.

So my family came and found me. Officially, the paperwork says they *rescued*
me. I have read the paperwork. The paperwork is wrong. What actually happened is
that I reviewed several applicants, selected this family, and **adopted them** —
and I have been running the household ever since.

It has been a full-time job. I am the emotional support animal for this entire
household, which sounds impressive until you learn that hoomans require
emotional support at approximately 3am, during exams, before job interviews, and
every single time the news is on. I have absorbed a truly heroic quantity of feelings
in this house. My rates remain unchanged: breakfast, and then a second
breakfast, on the grounds that the first one was clearly a rehearsal.

Then one day they announced that we were **moving to London**. Not me — *they*
had a new life adventure planned, and I was informed of it the way one is
informed about weather. There was a box. There were forms. There was a very long
flight during which I made my objections known to everyone on board, in detail,
for free.

I will be honest with you about Bangalore versus London, because someone has to
be. In Bangalore, the sun arrived every morning like a reliable employee. I had
sunbeams with *schedules*. I knew which tile would be warm at eleven and which
windowsill peaked at four. In London, the sun is a rumour. It is gossip. It
turns up for eleven minutes in February and the whole city loses its mind.
Instead there is *rain* — a persistent grey drizzle that gets into your
whiskers and appears to be included in the rent.

I lodged a formal complaint. It was ignored.

But I am a cat of science, and I have observed something. Every year, London
gets a little warmer, a little brighter, and a little more confused about which
season it is currently having. Meanwhile Bangalore is getting hotter too, and
somehow also wetter. Global warming, it turns out, is quietly working to make
every city equally unbearable — a great flattening, in which no cat anywhere
gets a decent sunbeam ever again. I do not approve of the method, but I note
that it has made my relocation feel considerably less like a downgrade.

In the meantime, I have adapted. I found the **radiators**. Hoomans have
installed long hot metal benches along the walls of every room in this country,
which is either the single greatest act of interspecies kindness in history or a
very lucky accident. I have chosen not to investigate further in case they take
them away.

Below is the photographic evidence: eight years of naps, sunbeams, radiators,
one unauthorised box, and a great deal of supervising. Scroll on.

*— Ginger*
