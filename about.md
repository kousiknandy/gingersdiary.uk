## The essentials

**Name:** Ginger. Also answers to Gingie, Ginguuu, a specific whistle, and the
sound of a pouch being opened from three rooms away.

**Age:** Eight. In cat years this makes me a distinguished senior professional.
In hooman years I am told this makes me "middle aged", which is rude.

**Colour:** Ginger. I refuse to elaborate.

**Occupation:** Emotional support animal, household supervisor, quality
assurance inspector for all warm surfaces.

**Born:** Bangalore, India. **Currently based:** London, United Kingdom, under
protest that has now softened into a kind of damp acceptance.

---

## How I acquired a family

I lost my mother when I was two weeks old. Before that happened, she did the one
thing she could still do for me: she picked out a hooman and made it very clear
that this hooman was now on duty. I have no memory of this negotiation. I am
assured it was firm.

What happened next gets described around here as a rescue. I want to formally
put on record that this is the hoomans' version of events. My version is that I
conducted a rapid assessment of the available candidates, identified one family
with a promising attitude to snacks and lap availability, and **adopted them**.
They have been mine ever since. They seem happy about it, which is the correct
response.

## The job

Being an emotional support animal is not, as the name suggests, a hobby. There
are shifts. There are on-call nights. When someone in this house has a bad day,
I am required to appear, sit on the relevant part of them, and radiate. When
someone is crying, I am required to arrive at speed and press my forehead into
their arm until it stops. When someone is doing something extremely important on
a laptop, I am required to sit on the laptop, because whatever it is, it is less
important than me and they will thank me later.

I do not get paid. I do get fed twice, sometimes three times, occasionally four
times if I work the shifts correctly and the hoomans do not compare notes.

## The great relocation

One day the hoomans announced that they were moving to London for a new
adventure. Note the pronoun. *They* were having an adventure. I was having a
carrier.

There were vet appointments. There were forms — an unbelievable number of forms,
none of which I was permitted to sit on. There was a flight, during which I
delivered a continuous, unedited review of the entire experience at volume, at
no charge, to everyone within earshot. I stand by every word.

And then the doors opened onto **London**, and I understood that a great crime
had been committed against me.

## A formal comparison of two cities

|                       | Bangalore | London |
|-----------------------|-----------|--------|
| Sunbeams              | Punctual, generous, daily | Rumoured |
| Rain                  | Dramatic, seasonal, over quickly | Ongoing. Ambient. Possibly structural |
| Warm floor tiles      | Standard issue | Absolutely not |
| Radiators             | Unheard of | **Everywhere.** See below |
| Birds outside window  | Loud, numerous, insulting | Damp, sarcastic |
| Verdict               | Correct | Under review |

In Bangalore I had a working knowledge of every warm surface in the building and
the exact hour each one peaked. It was a system. I had a whole *portfolio* of
sunbeams. In London the sun clocks in for eleven minutes in February and the
entire city goes outside to look at it.

## The radiators, however

I must be fair. London, having no sun to speak of, has compensated by installing
long hot metal benches along the walls of every single room. Nobody warned me
about this. I discovered the first one by accident and lay on it for four hours
in a state of religious awe.

I have decided not to ask any questions about the radiators in case someone
realises they were not meant for me.

## On the weather, generally

I have been keeping notes. London is getting warmer every year. Bangalore is
getting hotter, and also somehow wetter. The seasons have stopped introducing
themselves properly. From where I am sitting — usually a radiator — global
warming appears to be a large-scale project to make every city on earth equally
disappointing, so that no cat anywhere can claim to have the good sunbeams.

I want it on record that I did not vote for this. But I will admit it has made
London feel like less of a demotion, and I am too old and too warm to keep up a
grudge that size.

## Current status

Eight years old. Two countries. One family, thoroughly trained. Several
radiators, all of them mine.

Still accepting snacks.

*— Ginger*
